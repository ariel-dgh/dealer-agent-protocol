Methodology · Evidence before opinion

Thirty checks. Ten dimensions. One accountable work plan.

The framework evaluates whether AI systems can access, understand, corroborate, and accurately describe a dealership. Human review is applied to consequential findings and ambiguous evidence.

Scoring is deliberately strict.

Each check is scored Pass (2), Partial (1), or Fail (0). Missing evidence does not receive the benefit of the doubt. The total is directional—not a guarantee that any AI system will cite or recommend a dealership.

The ten dimensions

01 / ACCESS

AI crawler access

robots.txt, platform controls, WAF behavior, and real fetchability.

02 / GUIDANCE

AI discovery files

Curated llms.txt and useful paths without boilerplate or false claims.

03 / STRUCTURE

Schema and entities

AutoDealer, Vehicle, Product, LocalBusiness, FAQ, and consistent identifiers.

04 / IDENTITY

Entity reinforcement

Google Business Profile, OEM locators, maps, directories, and NAP consistency.

05 / ANSWERS

Conversational content

Direct answers to real buying, service, ownership, and local questions.

06 / AUTHORITY

Third-party citations

Local press, OEM recognition, industry sources, and trustworthy mentions.

07 / REPUTATION

Review evidence

Volume, recency, response quality, location accuracy, and topic coverage.

08 / FRESHNESS

Content freshness

Current hours, policies, staff, service information, inventory, and dated updates.

09 / EXPERIENCE

Technical quality

Rendered content, performance, mobile access, canonicalization, and crawl depth.

10 / OBSERVATION

AI answer spot-checks

Dated queries, mentions, citations, competitors, accuracy, and uncertainty.

How a finding becomes a work item

A failed check alone is not useful. Each material finding is documented as an evidence chain:

  1. Observation: what was actually returned, rendered, or found.
  2. Impact: why the issue can affect discovery, trust, or answer accuracy.
  3. Action: the smallest practical change that addresses the issue.
  4. Owner: dealer, website provider, agency, OEM, listings provider, or data layer.
  5. Verification: the test that proves the change was implemented.

Observed, inferred, and unavailable

The report separates direct observations from analyst inference. Platform outputs can vary by time, account, location, model, and access method. If a live engine cannot be tested reliably, the audit says so; it does not manufacture a result.

Score bands

  • 50–60: AEO-ready. Strong foundations with targeted improvement opportunities.
  • 35–49: Partially visible. Important signals exist, but material gaps remain.
  • 20–34: Foundation gaps. Structural issues are likely limiting visibility.
  • Below 20: Invisible. A foundational overhaul is indicated.
